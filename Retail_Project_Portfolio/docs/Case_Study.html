The problem

Deleting negative quantities hides cancellations. Inconsistent descriptions split product totals. Dropping missing customer IDs can remove valid sales.

The work

Processed 541,909 invoice lines, audited 5,268 exact-duplicate removals and excluded 2,512 nonpositive-price rows. Retained 534,129 lines and separated sales from C-invoice cancellations.

What the results show

  • Recorded cancellations equal 8.40% of gross value.
  • REGENCY CAKESTAND 3 TIER (22423) leads merchandise products by net value: £164,459.49.
  • 2,845 of 4,338 identified purchasing customers have at least two positive-sales invoices. Customer IDs cover 83.51% of gross value.

Purchase frequency

Repeat means two or more distinct positive-sales invoices in the observed window.

Suggested investigation priorities

  • Review large cancellation invoices and their original transactions before attributing the cause to stock or fulfilment problems.
  • Combine the top-product results with margin, inventory and lead-time data before changing stock levels.
  • Improve customer identification coverage before using repeat-purchase patterns to guide customer strategy.

These are investigation priorities, not measured business improvements.

Quality and scope

20 tests passed. An independent workbook calculation verified six analytical tables and 19,417 numeric values without pandas or production-code imports.

Historical data include wholesalers. Exact de-duplication is an assumption. Cancellations are not matched to original sales. Net recorded value includes nonstandard codes and is not accounting revenue, cash or profit. Repeat purchasing is not retention. December 2011 is partial.
